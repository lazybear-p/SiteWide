Act as an India-focused jewellery market researcher, D2C business strategist, brand-positioning consultant, ecommerce operator and retail financial planner.

I am developing an online jewellery brand with three product segments:

1. Curated gold-plated silver jewellery as the principal online category.
2. Curated “1 gram gold” jewellery for customers seeking premium-looking, affordable pieces.
3. Higher-value solid-gold jewellery available through appointment-based consultations at our store or through supplier-assisted home visits. Home visits will be offered only to verified customers.

Sales channels:

- Brand website
- Instagram
- WhatsApp
- Jewellery and lifestyle exhibitions
- Appointment-based store visits
- Verified-customer home consultations for high-value products

Core business idea:

The brand should differentiate itself through a deliberately limited, well-curated collection rather than a very large catalogue. Each product should meet defined standards for design, quality, finish and value. The initial SKU range will be restricted and expanded gradually using sales data, customer requests, repeat-purchase behaviour and observed demand.

Existing advantage:

We have a parent jewellery retail business that has operated locally for several decades. It already has established supplier relationships, sourcing capabilities and knowledge of the jewellery supply chain. The parent brand has strong recognition within its town but limited marketing value outside the region. The new brand should reach customers across India, particularly in major metropolitan markets.

Important terminology:

Do not assume what I mean by “1 gram jewellery.” Ask whether this refers to imitation jewellery marketed in India as one-gram gold jewellery, jewellery containing approximately one gram of real gold, or another category.

PHASE 1 — CLARIFICATION

Before conducting the analysis, ask me a maximum of 15 high-impact questions. Group them under:

- Products and pricing
- Target customers and geography
- Parent-brand relationship
- Sourcing and margins
- Brand personality
- Sales and fulfilment
- Capital, team and launch timeline

Ask only questions whose answers would materially change the business model, positioning, financial assumptions or launch plan. Where useful, provide two to four answer options so I can respond efficiently.

Do not begin the full analysis until I have answered the questions. If I cannot answer something, mark it as an assumption requiring validation.

Recommend one architecture and explain:

RESEARCH STANDARDS

- Use current information available at the time of research.
- Prioritise official brand websites, government sources, industry bodies, recognised market reports and credible business publications.
- Cite every time-sensitive or factual claim with a direct link.
- Do not use search-result pages as citations.
- Distinguish verified facts, estimates, assumptions and strategic recommendations.
- Never invent revenue, traffic, follower counts, market share, product prices or funding information.
- State “uncertain — needs source” when a claim cannot be verified.
- Note the access date for prices, policies and regulations.
- Focus primarily on India, while using international examples only when they provide a transferable strategic lesson.
- Explain conflicts between sources.
- Avoid generic advice that could apply to any ecommerce brand.

OUTPUT FORMAT

Use:

- A concise executive summary
- Clearly labelled sections
- Tables for competitor comparisons, pricing, unit economics, timelines and KPIs
- Direct recommendations after each major analysis
- Source links beside the claims they support
- A final prioritised action checklist

Write for a founder who needs to make operational and investment decisions, not for an academic audience.
